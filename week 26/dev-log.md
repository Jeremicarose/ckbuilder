## Overview

Week 26 focused on preparing the CKB Receiver Registry project for **pilot validation and potential Spark funding**.

The main focus shifted from additional technical development toward validating the product with real receiver operators and ecosystem coordinators. Work this week included researching suitable communities, defining the pilot cohort, establishing recruitment procedures, and refining the questions and evidence needed to determine whether the Registry solves a meaningful problem for its intended users.

### 2. Pilot Cohort Definition

Defined the pilot recruitment target as:

- **5 receiver operators**
- **3 coordinators/developers**
- **8 written confirmations**

The final pilot cohort will consist of:

- 4 receiver operators
- 2 coordinators/developers
- 2 backups

The cohort will prioritize participants from multiple independent receiver ecosystems.

### 3. Pilot Testing Structure

Defined the expected participant commitment:

- Up to **45 minutes** for product testing
- Up to **20 minutes** for an interview
- Short follow-up within **7 days**
- CKB testnet only
- No real funds required

The pilot will not request:

- Private keys
- Seed phrases
- Exchange credentials
- Receiver network credentials
- Private feed URLs
- Exact private receiver locations

### 4. Product Validation

Refined the central product-validation question:

> **Does a persistent, owner-controlled, cross-network receiver identity provide enough value over existing receiver/network-specific systems that an operator or coordinator would actually use or integrate with it?**

The research showed that existing platforms already provide some receiver registration, discovery, management, synchronization, and provenance functionality.

Therefore, the pilot will specifically test whether the CKB Receiver Registry provides meaningful additional value rather than simply duplicating existing directories.

### 5. Product Differentiation

Refined the product positioning around:

- Persistent receiver identity
- Owner-controlled identity
- Portable ownership
- Cross-network interoperability
- Receiver lifecycle history
- Verifiable provenance
- Clear active/revoked status

The pilot will test whether these capabilities are valuable enough to justify adoption or integration.

### 6. Pilot Evidence Framework

Defined the evidence that should be collected from participants, including:

- Problems participants have actually experienced
- Existing workarounds
- Time and coordination costs
- Observed usability during testing
- Valuable and unnecessary features
- Adoption blockers
- Integration requirements
- What CKB provides
- What CKB does not provide
- Concrete actions participants would take after the pilot

This will help distinguish genuine product demand from simple technical interest.
